Use Mercur to connect each seller to a payment provider and send them their share of every order.

A payout is the seller’s share of one order, sent from the marketplace to the seller’s account. The Payout module holds 4 records: the seller’s payout account, its onboarding state, the payouts made to it, and the reversals that take a refunded share back. Every external call goes through the one payout provider the marketplace registers. Stripe Connect ships as a plugin, and a built-in `system` provider runs when you register none.

<Note>
  Resolve the module service with `MercurModules.PAYOUT` from `@mercurjs/types`. Import the payout workflows from `@mercurjs/core/workflows`.
</Note>

## Key features

The Payout module covers a seller’s connection to the provider and the money sent through it:

- **One provider per marketplace**: the module loads exactly one `IPayoutProvider`, shaped like Medusa’s payment providers
- **Stripe Connect plugin**: `@mercurjs/stripe-connector-plugin` creates Express accounts, onboarding and login links, and pays each payout as a Connect transfer
- **One account per store**: `createPayoutAccountWorkflow` refuses a second account for the same seller
- **Re-runnable onboarding**: `createOnboardingWorkflow` creates the onboarding record once and updates it after that
- **One payout per order**: `createOrderPayoutsWorkflow` pays the order total minus the order’s commission lines, puts orders that aren’t ready on hold, and never pays an order twice
- **Reversals like refunds**: `createPayoutReversalsWorkflow` takes each later refund’s share back, net of its commission
- **Webhook-driven statuses**: provider webhooks refresh accounts and settle payouts through `processPayoutForWebhookWorkflow`

## How it works

A seller moves from no account to a paid order in 5 steps:

1. Run `createPayoutAccountWorkflow` (or `createOrRefreshSellerPayoutAccountWorkflow`) for the seller. The provider creates a connected account.
2. Run `createOnboardingWorkflow`. The provider returns onboarding data, such as a Stripe onboarding link.
3. The provider posts webhooks to `/hooks/payout`, and the `payout-webhook` subscriber re-reads the account from the provider and sets it to `pending`, `active`, `restricted`, or `rejected`.
4. Run `createOrderPayoutsWorkflow` with order ids. It computes each seller’s share and asks the provider to pay it.
5. Run `createPayoutReversalsWorkflow` after refunds. Provider webhooks update payout statuses along the way.

<Warning>
  `@mercurjs/core` ships no scheduled jobs, so it doesn’t decide when steps 4 and 5 run. The Stripe connector plugin brings its own schedule. Without it, call the workflows from your own job, subscriber, or route. See [The payout pipeline](/platform/payout/concepts/payout-pipeline).
</Warning>
