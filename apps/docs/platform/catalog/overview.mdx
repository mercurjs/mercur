Use Mercur to keep one product catalog for the whole marketplace and control which stores can sell each product.

The catalog is Medusa's Product module (`Modules.PRODUCT`) with a marketplace layer on top. Products, variants, categories, collections, tags, and types are native Medusa models. Mercur adds the `product_seller` allowlist, a review flow over the product `status`, an audit record for every submission, and its own product workflows. No store owns a product: a store sells a master product through an [offer](/platform/offer/overview) that carries its own SKU, price, and stock.

<Note>
  There's no Mercur catalog module. Resolve the service with `Modules.PRODUCT` from `@medusajs/framework/utils`, and run Mercur's product workflows from `@mercurjs/core/workflows`. Mercur's `createProductsWorkflow` has the same name as Medusa's, so check the import path.
</Note>

## Key features

The catalog layer covers how products enter the marketplace and who can sell them:

- **Shared master products**: one `Product` record per item, sold by any number of stores through offers
- **Store allowlist**: the `product_seller` link restricts a product to the stores on it; a product with no rows is open to every store
- **Review flow**: vendor submissions start as `proposed` and an operator publishes or rejects them
- **Submission audit**: every create and review step writes a confirmed `ProductChange` record with the actor who did it
- **Attribute-driven variants**: products take Mercur attributes on create, and variant-axis attributes become the options variants bind to
- **Native structure**: variants, categories, collections, tags, and types come straight from Medusa's Product module

## How it works

A product moves through the catalog in a fixed order:

1. A store or an operator runs `createProductsWorkflow`. Vendor submissions default to `proposed`.
2. The workflow records a `PRODUCT_ADD` change with the creator as `created_by`, and links any `seller_ids` to the product.
3. An operator runs `confirmProductsWorkflow`, `rejectProductWorkflow`, or `requestProductChangeWorkflow` on the proposed product.
4. Once `published`, the product shows in the Store API and to every store its allowlist permits.
5. Each store that sells it creates an offer against one of its variants.
