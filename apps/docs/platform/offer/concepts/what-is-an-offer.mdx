An offer is a store’s listing of one master product variant. The catalog product belongs to the marketplace, and the offer holds the store’s commercial terms on top of it: SKU, barcodes, condition, shipping profile, prices, and stock. You need this split in mind before you build anything that sells, because carts, orders, commissions, and payouts all follow the offer, not the product.

## The offer record

The `Offer` data model (table `offer`, id prefix `offer`) is a thin row that points at catalog records by id. Prices and inventory aren’t columns on it: they attach through module links.

The row carries these groups of fields:

- **Ownership**: `seller_id` is the store, and `created_by` is the member who created the offer
- **Catalog pointers**: `variant_id` and `product_id`, where `product_id` is read from the variant when the offer is created
- **Identity**: `sku`, plus optional `ean` and `upc` barcodes copied from the variant when you leave them out
- **Selling terms**: `shipping_profile_id`, `manage_inventory` (default `true`), `allow_backorder` (default `false`), and `leadtime_to_ship`
- **Condition**: an optional `condition_id` that points at an `OfferCondition`

```ts
const { data: [offer] } = await query.graph({
  entity: "offer",
  fields: ["id", "sku", "seller.name", "product_variant.title"],
  filters: { id: "offer_01JD8Q1234567" },
})
```

This query reads one offer with its store and master variant. `seller` and `product_variant` resolve through read-only links on `seller_id` and `variant_id`.

## Offers and master products

A master variant can back any number of offers. Each store that sells the variant holds its own offer, and the variant itself never changes when a store lists, prices, or stocks it.

The same store can also list a variant more than once, for example a new and a refurbished copy. The module doesn’t limit offers per variant. The only uniqueness rule is the SKU.

<Tip>
  A SKU is unique within a store: the index on `(seller_id, sku)` applies while `deleted_at` is null. Two stores can use the same SKU string, and a soft-deleted offer frees its SKU.
</Tip>

## Offer conditions

An offer condition describes the physical state an offer is sold in. Operators manage conditions, and stores pick one when they create or update an offer.

The `OfferCondition` model (id prefix `ofcond`) has a unique `code`, a display `label`, an `is_active` flag, and a `rank` that orders the list. The create and update workflows check every `condition_id` they receive:

- **Unknown id**: the run fails with a not found error
- **Inactive condition**: the run fails, so a deactivated condition can’t be assigned to new or edited offers
- **Condition in use**: `deleteOfferConditionsWorkflow` refuses to delete a condition while any offer still uses it

Deactivating a condition leaves the offers already listed under it unchanged.

## Grouping offers by store

A store’s offers on one product usually mirror that product’s variants. The service can collapse them into one row per product and store.

When you pass `group_by_seller: true` to `listOffers` or `listAndCountOffers`, the service returns the newest offer of each `(product_id, seller_id)` group and sets two computed fields on it:

- **`variant_count`**: how many offers the store has on that product
- **`offer_ids`**: the ids of all offers in the group

Only `product_id` and `seller_id` filters apply in grouped mode.
